# Flint — Startup Pitch

---

## The One-Liner

> Flint is the first tool that works for developers even when they're not using it.

---

## The Problem

There are two kinds of problems developers have.

The first kind they know about. Bugs. Errors. Security vulnerabilities. Slow queries. Bad patterns. There are hundreds of tools for these. The market is saturated.

The second kind they don't know about.

The mental model of their codebase quietly draining. Decision fatigue accumulating invisibly by 3pm. Knowledge silos forming without anyone deciding to form them. Technical debt growing not in big choices but in six weeks of small ones. Documentation wrong the moment it's written. Burnout approaching long before it arrives. Career blind spots widening because nobody is watching what they're never doing.

These problems have no tools. They don't even have names.

And they are the ones that actually break codebases and end careers.

---

## The Insight

Every developer tool today is reactive.

You open it. You ask it something. It answers. You close it and go back to work.

The intelligence only exists when you remember to use it.

Which means the most important problems — the ones you don't know you have — are never caught. You can't ask about the bug you don't know exists. You can't prompt for the pattern you've been repeating for six months. You don't think to check for burnout until you're already in it.

The tools aren't broken. The model is broken.

---

## The Solution

Flint is passive technical and human intelligence that lives inside your editor and terminal.

It doesn't wait to be asked. It watches your work — your code, your commits, your patterns, your behaviour — and speaks up when it has something worth saying. The way a great senior developer would if they were always available, always paying attention, and never annoying.

It operates on two planes simultaneously:

**Technical intelligence** — code quality, security, architecture signals, codebase health, knowledge silos, debt accrual rate, documentation decay. The things a senior dev would catch before they become problems.

**Human intelligence** — energy patterns, burnout early signals, career blind spots, interruption cost, cognitive load accumulation, growth and stagnation. The things a senior dev who actually cares about you would notice before you do.

Both planes. One presence. Same voice. Same sidebar. Indistinguishable in tone.

---

## The Product

Flint lives in VS Code, Cursor, and Windsurf as a slim sidebar presence. It runs as a background process in the terminal. It watches everything you allow it to watch.

Most of the time, it is silent. A 4px strip on the side of your editor. Almost invisible.

When it has something worth saying — it appears. One card. Senior dev voice. Plain language. Then it fades. No chat window. No input box. No interface to manage.

When it spots something fixable, it hands you a ready-to-use prompt for your AI agent — formatted for Cursor, Antigravity, Windsurf, or whatever agent you use. One tap to copy. One tap to send. The observation and the fix in a single moment.

Three awareness levels — **Spark** (see only what you show it), **Flame** (know this project), **Forge** (know everything) — give developers full control over how much Flint sees. The human intelligence layer is opt-in separately. Every byte of data stays local. Nothing leaves the machine.

---

## Why Now

Three things converged to make Flint possible today that weren't true two years ago:

**AI that sounds human.** The quality of language models has crossed a threshold where the output can genuinely sound like a senior colleague, not a system warning. That's not a small thing — it's the entire product.

**The AI editor moment.** Cursor, Antigravity, Windsurf, VS Code Copilot — AI-native editors are now where developers live. The infrastructure for a passive presence inside the editor already exists. Flint slots into it.

**Developer burnout is at a historic high.** Post-layoff survivor syndrome, smaller teams doing more work, the expectation of constant shipping. Developers are burning out faster and earlier than at any point in the industry's history. The market for something that actually watches out for them — technically and humanly — has never been larger.

---

## The Moat

Every version of Flint that ships makes the next version harder to replicate.

From day one, Flint is building a personal model of each developer — their patterns, their blind spots, their habits, their mistakes. By month three, Flint knows things about how a developer works that no tool has ever known. By month six, it has caught things that would have taken months to surface any other way.

You don't leave that. You can't take it with you. The longer you use Flint, the more it knows, the more useful it becomes. That is a compounding moat — not a feature moat, a data moat. One that lives on the developer's machine and belongs entirely to them.

## The Business Model

Flint rolls out completely free until developers can't imagine working without it. That is the only bar that matters before charging.

**Free rollout — 12 months**
Every feature. Every awareness level. Every tool. Free. The goal is trust, retention, and the kind of word-of-mouth that only happens when something is genuinely indispensable.

**Monetisation triggers (both must be true)**
30-day retention above 40%. At least 10 unprompted "I can't work without this" from developers who were not asked.

**Pricing when ready**
- Spark — free forever (manual tools, no daemon)
- Flame — $9/month (daemon, pre-commit checkpoints, full toolkit)
- Forge — $18/month (everything, human intelligence layer, cross-project memory)
- Team — $15/seat/month (Forge for everyone + team features)

Every developer active during the free rollout gets grandfathered at Forge, free, forever. Non-negotiable. It is what turns free users into advocates.

At 1% of the addressable market paying — $32M ARR. At 5% — $162M.
